# Minsky Home: AI-Native Repositioning

*Copy and visual direction · Draft v1 · October 2026*

## 1. The idea in one line

Most studios *say* “AI-native.” Minsky’s home page should *behave* AI-native.

So the home page stops being a brochure with stacked sections. It becomes **one continuous session**: it listens to the visitor, composes something for them, shows its working, and hands every decision back to a human. The visitor should feel they’ve touched software that is alive, calm, and clearly made by people who care.

**Design thesis: Two Voices.**

- **The machine voice** is monospace, green-phosphor, fast, streaming. It proposes, drafts, and routes.
- **The human voice** is a warm serif, ink-dark, slower, deliberate. It decides, rejects, and rewrites.

Every scene on the page shows the two collaborating, with the human always having the last word. That is your philosophy (AI-native *and* an act of care) made visible rather than stated.

It also grows out of what you already own: the terminal `$_` motif, the monospace type, and the green grid. We are evolving the brand, not replacing it.

## 2. What we are deliberately avoiding

As requested: no orbs, no nodes, no brains, no networks. Also no glowing purple-blue gradients, no sparkle icons, no floating robot or chat-bubble mascots, no “neural” particle backgrounds, and no stock “AI revolution” language. These are the visual clichés that make every AI site look identical. Our differentiator is **calm and craft**, not spectacle.

## 3. Page architecture: a session, not sections

There are no hard section breaks or boxed cards. The page is one scroll, told as scenes that resolve into one another. The nine lifecycle icons from the current hero (Ideation → Analytics) become a **thin vertical spine** down the left edge that lights up stage by stage as you scroll. It works as a progress indicator, a table of contents, and a nod to your existing identity.

| Scene | Job it does | Signature moment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Arrival | Position + hook | Visitor types a problem, gets a live brief |
| 1. Philosophy | Values | A manifesto being redlined by AI and a human |
| 2. What AI-native means | Differentiate | A UI that morphs from “bolted-on” to “native” |
| 3. Capabilities | Offer | A live work ledger instead of service cards |
| 4. How we work | Process + trust | A commit log with human gates |
| 5. Proof | Credibility | Case “traces” of real systems |
| 6. Sustainability | Cost / open source | A cost slider |
| 7. Close | Convert | Contact as a typed conversation |

### Scene 0: Arrival

**What the visitor sees:** an almost empty dark-paper screen. One breathing caret. The headline types itself in the human serif. Then a mono line streams beneath it.

**Eyebrow (mono, small):** `minsky / AI-native software studio / Chennai`

**Headline (recommended):**

> Software that works quietly.

**Alternates to test:**

- AI-native software, built with care.
- Built to fade into the background.
- Software that takes care of things.

**Machine line (streams in):**

`AI-native. Human-centric. Open at heart.`

**Sub-copy:**

We design, build, and look after software where intelligence is the foundation, not a feature. It serves people without demanding their attention.

**The prompt (the hero’s real interaction):**

A single input where the old CTA button used to be.

- Placeholder: *What’s taking too much of your team’s time?*
- Suggestion chips: *A CRM that follows up on its own* · *Cut our manual approvals* · *An app for 10,000 field workers* · *Make our data answer questions*
- Small link beneath: *or just talk to a human →*

**What happens on submit:** the page streams a short first-draft brief, in the machine voice, in five parts:

1. **What we heard** (their problem, restated)
2. **Where AI should do the work**
3. **Where a person should decide**
4. **A sensible shape** (open-source stack, rough timeline, rough cost range)
5. **What we’d ask you next**

It closes in the human voice:

> Drafted by AI in a few seconds. Reviewed by a person at Minsky within one working day. **\[ Send this to the team \]**

This is the strongest proof of “AI-native” on the page: the site does what we sell. It is also a lead-capture mechanism far better than a Contact button.

### Scene 1: Philosophy as a redlined manifesto

**Label:** `/philosophy`

The scene looks like a document being edited. Human lines are serif. Machine suggestions appear as mono redlines. The human accepts or rejects each, with a short note. Three beats:

1. **Machine suggests:** *make it stickier, drive engagement.* **Human rejects:** *“No. Attention isn’t the product.”*
2. **Machine suggests:** *standardise on a proprietary platform.* **Human rewrites:** *“Open source, so you can leave whenever you want.”*
3. **Machine suggests:** *scale fast.* **Human rewrites:** *“Scale without breaking the bank.”*

When the redlines settle, the full statement is left on screen, large:

> For us, building software is an act of care. Care for the people who use it, and for the people who will own and sustain it long after it’s built.
>
> We believe in building sustainable, human-centric, AI-native software grounded in open-source values and thoughtful engineering.
>
> Our goal is to create software that evolves with people, scales without breaking the bank, and reliably serves human needs without demanding attention for itself.

This lets the AI-native claim land through values instead of buzzwords: the machine is fast, the human has taste.

### Scene 2: What “AI-native” actually means

**Headline:**

> AI-native is not a chatbot in the corner.

**Body:**

Most software adds AI the way a building adds a sign. We start with intelligence as the engine. It reads, routes, drafts, and handles the routine, and we design everything else around the people who stay in charge.

**Interaction:** a two-state toggle, *Bolted-on* / *Native*, that morphs one real-looking CRM screen.

- **Bolted-on:** a fourteen-field lead form, dense tabs, and a small “Ask AI” button in the corner.
- **Native:** the fields dissolve. One quiet line remains: `Priya from Coord asked about pricing 2 hours ago. Reply drafted from your last three proposals.` **\[ Send \] \[ Edit \] \[ Not now \]** Beneath it, dimmer: `11 other things handled. Nothing else needs you.`

**Caption:**

> The best interface is the one you rarely have to open.

**Three principles (set as one typographic line each, not cards):**

- **Intelligence is the engine.** It drives decisions, workflows, and automation, not just the chat box.
- **Humans hold the pen.** AI proposes. People approve anything that matters.
- **Quiet by default.** No nagging, no notification theatre. Software that fades into the background.

### Scene 3: What we put to work (capabilities)

**Label:** `/capabilities`

Instead of three cards, a mono **ledger**. Each row is a line of work, and rows expand in place with a short human-written description.

**01 / AI-native products** Applications built from the ground up with AI as the core engine, where intelligence drives decisions, workflows, automation, and the whole experience.

**02 / Intelligent systems for how you already work** *(proposed new offer)* Your CRM, your approvals, your inbox, your reporting, re-founded on AI without a risky rewrite of everything.

**03 / Design & full-stack foundations** Clean, fast, accessible interfaces and scalable, secure platforms for the intelligence to stand on. Web design and end-to-end product engineering, with AI in the loop.

**04 / Tending** *(proposed new offer)* Evaluations, cost monitoring, model upgrades, and maintenance, so what we build stays sharp, affordable, and yours to run.

Note the reordering: **AI-Native Apps leads** (it is already first in your site’s capabilities list, but buried below the philosophy). Web design moves from headline to foundation.

### Scene 4: How we work (process as a commit log)

**Headline:**

> Small steps, in the open, with a human at every gate.

A scrolling git-style log. Machine steps are green. Human gates are marked `◐` and appear in the serif.

```
day 01   understand   sit with the people who will actually use it
day 03   prototype    something working, not slides
  ◐      gate         you try it. you tell us what’s wrong.
day 10   evaluate     test the AI on your real cases; measure its mistakes
  ◐      gate         a person signs off on what it may do alone
day 14   ship v1      to a small group first
ongoing  tend         watch cost, quality and drift
```

*Timelines are placeholders. Replace with your real cadence.*

**Beneath the log, three promises:**

- **You own it.** Code, prompts, evaluations, and data.
- **No lock-in.** Model-agnostic, self-hostable, open-source where it matters.
- **No heroics.** Built so an ordinary team can maintain it.

### Scene 5: Proof (case traces)

**Label:** `/in-the-wild`

Each case study from your Work page becomes a short “trace” (a replay of what the system does) rather than a thumbnail. Use a four-line format:

- **The situation**
- **What we built**
- **What it does now** (what the system handles on its own)
- **What stayed human** (this line is what makes it distinctive)

**Starter copy, built from your current descriptions:**

- **Coord:** A dynamic website and a simple CRM for a craft community platform. *\[Add what the system now handles automatically, if true.\]*
- **TNGSS:** A feature-rich mobile app for networking and navigation. *\[Add intelligent features, if any.\]*
- **ICDIC:** Helping MSMEs find incentives, schemes, and expert facilitation. *\[Strong candidate for an AI matching story, if it exists.\]*
- **TVK:** A comprehensive digital presence and CMS for a political party and its ecosystem.

Below the traces, a quiet, slow-moving client line: FaMeTN, ICDIC, TNGSS, Coord, Filmster, Startup TN, Prime Public School, BeInspace. The government and ecosystem names carry real trust, so give them more weight than a typical logo strip.

**Important:** only claim AI behaviour that is true for each project. If a past project wasn’t AI-native, present it as *“the foundations we’ve been laying”* rather than overclaiming.

### Scene 6: Sustainability (the cost slider)

**Headline:**

> Scales without breaking the bank.

**Body:**

Open-source where it matters. Smaller models where they’re enough. The biggest models only where they earn their cost.

**Interaction:** a single draggable slider for *monthly actions* (from 1,000 to 1,000,000). Two lines recalculate live: an indicative monthly run cost on a Minsky-style open, model-routed stack versus a typical all-proprietary setup. The point is a gap that visibly widens as scale grows.

*Use only numbers you can defend. Label them “indicative” and link to a short methodology note.*

### Scene 7: Close (contact as conversation)

**Headline:**

> Let’s build something that takes care.

The form is replaced by a typed conversation, one question at a time:

1. *What are you building, or trying to fix?*
2. *Who will use it?*
3. *How should we reach you?*

**Confirmation:**

> Got it. A person will reply within one working day. Not a bot.

Keep phone, email, LinkedIn, Instagram, X, Terms, and Privacy in the footer as they are today.

## 4. Visual direction

**Overall feel:** a quiet, intelligent instrument. More Teenage Engineering manual and good terminal than sci-fi movie. Restraint is the premium signal.

### Palette

- **Ink:** near-black with a warm tint (`#0E0E0C`) for the dark state
- **Bone:** warm off-white (`#ECE8DF`) for the light state
- **Phosphor:** your existing green, tuned slightly toward lime (around `#B6F35B`). It is used *only* for the machine voice and live states.
- **Ember:** a small warm amber (around `#F2A33A`) used *only* for human moments such as approvals, gates, and the human’s notes.

Green means “the machine is working.” Amber means “a person is deciding.” Nothing else is coloured.

### Typography (two voices, plus UI)

- **Human voice:** a characterful serif for headlines and decisions (candidates: Instrument Serif, Newsreader, Fraunces)
- **Machine voice:** a crisp monospace (Geist Mono, JetBrains Mono, or keep your current mono)
- **UI / body:** a neutral grotesk (Geist, Inter Tight)

The serif is the biggest change from today’s geometric sans, and it is what stops the page reading as “another dark tech site.”

### Imagery and texture

No stock imagery and no illustrations of “AI.” The visuals are the product itself: live type, UI fragments, diffs, logs, and traces. The only decorative texture is a fine paper grain and the faint phosphor grid you already have, which now reacts subtly to the cursor.

### Motion principles

- **Things resolve; they don’t fly in.** Text streams character by character (about 30 to 50 ms), and UI fragments sharpen from soft to crisp.
- **Variable-font “breathing.”** The headline’s weight shifts very slightly as you move or pause, like something alive but at rest.
- **Calm attention.** The paragraph you are reading stays bright and the rest of the page dims. This embodies “doesn’t demand attention.”
- **Rhythm, not speed.** Machine moments are quick. Human moments are deliberately slower. The tempo difference tells the story.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` fully, with a static, equally good version.

### Navigation

Remove the pill nav bar. Replace it with a minimal wordmark at top left and a **command bar** fixed at bottom centre:

`⌘K  Ask Minsky  ·  Work  ·  Services  ·  Products  ·  Blog  ·  Stack  ·  Contact`

It opens as a command palette (jump anywhere, or ask a question). Every page in your current IA stays reachable.

### Adaptive touches (use sparingly)

- **Light and dark by local time.** The page opens in Bone by day and Ink by night.
- **Role lens (optional chips under the hero):** *Founder · Government / institution · Operations lead · Developer.* Choosing one rewrites the sub-copy. For example, the Government lens says: *“Software that serves citizens without adding to your team’s workload, built on open standards you can audit and own.”*

### Mobile

The prompt input stays the hero. Scenes stack vertically, the spine becomes a thin top progress line, and the command bar becomes a single bottom pill.

## 5. Build and safety notes

- **Server-render all copy** for SEO and no-JS visitors. The live demos should enhance the page, not be required for it.
- **The hero prompt uses a real model** with streaming, a strict system prompt, rate limiting, and a spending cap. Clearly label output as AI-drafted.
- **Privacy matches the philosophy:** don’t store prompts unless the visitor chooses “Send this to the team,” and say so in plain words.
- **Performance budget:** fast first paint (target LCP under 2 seconds on mid-range phones). Lazy-load the demos and keep the initial JavaScript lean.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG AA contrast in both themes, full keyboard path through the command bar, and screen-reader-friendly streaming (announce on completion, not per character).
- **Fallback:** if the model call fails, show a pre-written sample brief so the hero never feels broken.

## 6. Meta, navigation, and microcopy

- **Page title:** Minsky: AI-native software, built with care
- **Meta description:** Minsky is an AI-native software studio. We build sustainable, human-centric software on open-source foundations that serves people without demanding their attention.
- **Primary CTA:** *Send this to the team*
- **Secondary CTA:** *Talk to a human*
- **Nav labels (unchanged):** Home, Works, Services, Products, Blog, Stack, Contact

## 7. Assumptions and things to confirm

1. **Timelines, costs, and “one working day” replies** are placeholders. Only publish what you will honour.
2. **“Intelligent systems for existing workflows” and “Tending”** are offers I proposed to make the AI-native position credible. Keep them only if you’ll sell them.
3. **Case studies** need honest AI-contribution lines. Where there isn’t one, say so.
4. Your live site’s hero text appears to rotate between “Websites” and “CRM.” The new positioning replaces that artifact entirely.
5. The cost slider needs a defensible methodology before launch.

## 8. If you only build three things

1. **The hero prompt that returns a live brief** (the clearest proof of AI-native, and your best lead engine).
2. **The redlined manifesto** (turns values into the memorable moment).
3. **The bolted-on vs native toggle** (explains what “AI-native” means in five seconds, with no jargon).

The Two Voices palette and type system will do the rest of the differentiating on its own.
